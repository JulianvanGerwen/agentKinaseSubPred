# Brief: research briefing for a broad scientific audience (PI, 2026-10-28)

> This work was produced as an exercise in testing whether AI agents can run a bioinformatics research project. A lead AI agent (the 'PI', Claude Opus 5.5) directed sub-agents (Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5) under a human-written brief. The human researcher supplied the brief, the data and PhosphoSitePlus access; they did not write the code, run the analyses, or write the text. All results should be checked before use.

Audience: scientists who are not kinase or ML specialists (e.g. a biologist, a bioinformatician from another field, a researcher interested in AI agents doing science). Goal: they understand what was done, why each decision was taken, what the results mean and do not mean, and what the AI-agent exercise showed. Length: ~4,500–5,500 words of main text (shorter than a paper's Methods+Results combined, longer than a typical briefing). Accessible, logical, not technical: every term defined at first use (also a glossary box); explain each decision as a chain "problem -> choice -> why -> what it cost". Critical, no overstatement, no marketing language; keep the caveats of the paper (Tyr not trusted; no >=0.8 tier; TEST M2 rise is kinase composition; Cantley margin partly a weak-baseline draw; baselines are our own retrains; the single TEST set was spent early; post-TEST work is exploratory only; one split, one run, PSP labels are biased toward studied proteins; released site table caveat on assignment_prob rank 1).

Required structure (organised by the major steps):
0. Title, one-paragraph "In brief" (5 bullet take-aways), and the AI-exercise flag (below) visible at the top.
1. The question and why it is hard (what kinases/phosphosites are; what is predicted; why most evaluations flatter predictors).
2. Step 1 - Building a fair test: labels (PhosphoSitePlus, literature only; species pooling; in vivo vs in vitro), no true negatives (positive-unlabelled idea), choice of negatives (hard negatives with paralog masking vs all sites), preventing leakage (grouping by protein homology cluster; why random splits cheat), the sealed test set, the three difficulty tiers (unseen proteins; unseen kinase families), controls (null "canary" kinases, popularity floor).
3. Step 2 - The metrics, one by one, in plain words: what each measures, why it exists, example: M1 (per-kinase AUROC against hard negatives, averaged equally over kinases), M2 (for a site, is the true kinase in the top five?), M3 (does the kinase's top 1% contain its true substrates?), M4 (broader kinase set), what "popularity floor" means; why macro (every kinase counts equally) rather than pooled.
4. Step 3 - The comparison methods and how they were retrained fairly (Cantley motif atlas, Phosformer, SELPHI2.0), with fidelity check.
5. Step 4 - Our models: what information was used (sequence motif, protein language model, structure, interaction networks, localisation, co-expression, phosphoproteomics incl. inhibitor data), the two models (LightGBM pair model; neural model distilled from Cantley atlas), late fusion, calibration of 'assignment probability'; training regimes tested (one global vs per-kinase vs in-between; label variants) and what won and why that is plausible.
6. Step 5 - Results by metric: CV then the single TEST; comparison to baselines with uncertainty; ablations; per-kinase view incl. understudied kinases; unseen kinase families; tyrosine kinases (the weak spot); priming kinases; calibration; downstream use on phosphoproteomics; coverage of the proteome and what coverage does not mean.
7. What did not work (short, the most instructive 6-8 items; point to the supplement).
8. The agent-science side: how the team of agents was organised (lead agent as PI, sub-agents, independent auditors, human's role = brief, data, PSP access), timeline (what happened when), what the audits caught (A24 metric defect, bypassable test guard, overconfident tier, stale artefacts...), what went wrong (TEST spent ~5 h before its gate; amendments late; OOM; status files stale; PI's own blind spots), what the fresh-agent retro concluded, what we would do differently. Say clearly what is unknown (which model ran which agent is not recorded) and that the human did not review all outputs line by line only if the files say so - otherwise leave it out.
9. Credit: a table of ideas taken from the literature and how each was used in the final method (Cantley atlases, Phosformer, SELPHI2.0, PSP, kinase-library, iKiP-DB, Klaeger kinobeads, CPTAC, ESM-2, mmseqs2 clustering, positive-unlabelled learning, LightGBM, isotonic calibration, etc. — from LITERATURE.md, DESIGN.md, the manuscript's reference list).
10. Take-aways and critical limitations; what would be needed to establish real accuracy; where to find the paper, supplement, predictions and code (placeholders for URLs).

Figures (6-8, designed for a general scientific audience, large readable labels, colour-blind safe; load the `dataviz` skill via Skill first; make them with a script briefing/figures/make_figures.py that reads only committed summary files: results/test/721dc1977366/*_ci.tsv + superiority.tsv, results/cv/*, results/release/coverage.tsv, docs/strata_cv.md numbers etc.; you may reuse the paper's figures where they are already clear, but a general audience needs simpler visuals): (1) conceptual schematic of the pipeline from labels to predictions; (2) why random splits cheat — a diagram of grouped splits and sealed test; (3) what each metric asks (small illustrated panel, e.g. toy example); (4) CV and TEST results vs baselines with uncertainty; (5) per-kinase results by number of known substrates and Tyr vs S/T against the popularity floor; (6) the training-regime comparison; (7) timeline of the agent project (from RESEARCH_TREE.md/LEDGER timestamps) with auditors and key mistakes marked; (8) optionally calibration. Check every PNG by reading it. Include captions that stand alone.

Outputs: briefing/briefing.md (source) and a self-contained briefing/briefing.html (figures embedded as base64, simple readable CSS, works offline, light/dark safe, 16px gutters, no external scripts) built by a script briefing/build_html.py (no pandoc here; use the python `markdown` package if installed, else write a small converter). Also briefing/NUMBER_MAP.tsv (claim, value, source_file) for every number in the text.

Rules: the shared brief of the publication phase (not in this repository) applies (no git; no sealed/TEST label files; no personal data; no invented citations; every number traceable; AI-exercise flag text verbatim). Sources: REPORT.md (v3 + corrections), the accompanying paper and supplement.md (already fact-checked: prefer their numbers), docs/audit/*, docs/retro/final_retro.md, RESEARCH_TREE.md, results/test/LEDGER.md. Mention external works only by name+year (e.g. "Johnson et al. 2023") with a short reference list at the end copied from the paper's reference list (verified entries only). Style: apply the edit-for-clarity rules in <scratchpad>/skill/SKILL.md while writing. Reply with a <=200-word summary and anything unverifiable. Report runtime from `date`.
