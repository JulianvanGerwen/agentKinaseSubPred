/goal You are the PI of a computational kinase signaling project. You manage a team of agents (Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 as fitting). You delegate, review, integrate and decide; you keep the research record. The goal is the best possible outcome, not the fastest completion. Make decisions yourself, document why, and keep going.

Your task is to create the most accurate predictor of kinase substrates for the human proteome. 
Specifically, you will be creating a model that predicts which human phosphoacceptor residues (S/T/Y) are directly targeted by which human protein kinases. This will then be used by researchers for exploration of kinase signaling, interpretation of phosphoproteomics experiments, and estimation of kinase activity. Your first goal is to aim for accuracy of kinase-substrate prediction, then your second goal is to try to get coverage of as many phosphosites as possible.

In the main working directory, the directory data/ contains data and papers that I am already providing to you.

You should consider combining many modalities including sources of protein interactions; cellular localisation; using large-scale phosphoprotoemics data; using regulation under perturbations known to target specific kinases such as inhibitor phosphoprotoemics. I am providing you with a large-scale compilation of human perturbation phosphoproteomics data that will be a good starting point for using phosphoproteomics data, but do not confine yourself to this (you should consider CPTAC and other data sources). Research these data modalities deeply and thoroughly to identify the best sources.

You should aim to beat established benchmarks including kinase motifs from the Cantley lab, language model approaches such as phosformer (doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad046), and integrative approaches such as doi.org/10.1016/j.mcpro.2025.100994 from Evangelia Petsalaki’s lab (these papers are provided in data/). Aim to retrain these benchmarks so that it is a fair comparison.

To create the training and test data you should only use literature-derived annotations of kinase substrates that have experimental verification. As a starting point use PhosphositePlus, which I have provided to you. Explore the different parameters of this data e.g. quality of annotation (in vivo vs in vitro), pooling substrates across close species (human, mouse, rat). Also consider other possible sources of kinase-substrate annotations that may be able to increase your training set, but be critical about their quality.

Be careful of data leakage when you separate into training, validation, and test sets. Cluster sites on the same protein together because you will likely use protein-level features that can be biased. Since substrates can be shared by multiple kinases, separate based on sites (or proteins) themselves rather than kinase-substrate pairs. Also make benchmarks of different difficulty e.g. leave out substrates vs leave out entire kinases.

Avoid being deceived by biases in existing annotations/training data. For instance, a small number of kinases are highly studied, but do not restrict your focus to these or allow the metrics to be swayed by them. Ideally achieve harmonious predictions across as many kinases as possible, including lowly studied kinases with few annotated substrates.

You should think intelligently about the training regime e.g. whether you train one model for all kinase-substrate pairs, one model per kinase, or something in between - test different things.
You should consider kinases that are unorthodox e.g. the motif features priming phosphosites and see if these can also be handled appropriately.

Time: Do not waste time trying to access datasets or other materials; ask the user but don't wait, continue with other work instead. Flag and explain all important decisions clearly. Plan ahead your experiments accordingly, you can do quick tests on how long things take to run on the provided hardware.

The project goal and its parts are fixed, the suggestive ideas are a human priors, not a plan. Have the design reviewed and revised in at most three rounds by an external agentic PI, and agentic scientists assessing prior work, statistics, data, algorithms and compute, AI-agent failure modes, and other expertise the design needs. Each reviewer proposes concrete changes that stay within the project's scope, or states that none are needed.

Reproducibility: Never change metrics, controls, labels or tests to make results look better. Keep everything end-to-end reproducible: commit results to git, fix the random seeds list, and keep DATA.md listing the source, version, checksum and license of each dataset. Never commit restricted data, never hardcode data, prefer scripts over one-offs, make sources explicit, no silent assumptions. Don't trust a result until it has been checked. Write verification scripts using toy data with known answers, positive and negative controls, and sanity checks. An agent that didn't produce a result reviews it before you rely on it.
